## Numbered findings

### 1. HIGH: Universal verification wording exceeds the reviewable record

Rules: R-35 (verify before delivery), C-5 (evidence over claims).

Location: page 7, final paragraph; `assessment/build_report.py:187`. Related evidence: `assessment/DESIGN-QA.md`, `web/e2e/review.spec.ts`, `web/e2e/walkthrough.spec.ts`, and `web/src/pages/fitgap/FitGapReportPage.test.tsx`.

One-line reason: The report says every new review control has a tested action, but the submitted record does not provide the complete element-by-element click-through required to substantiate that universal claim.

What is supported: the tests operate evidence disclosure, follow-up filtering, refresh, retry, downloads, transcript navigation, initial-load retry, and Check status recovery. The recorded workflow enters the comparison from the portfolio.

What is missing from that record: the two return-to-portfolio links are present in source but are not operated in the inspected browser or frontend tests. The submitted design gate does not include the mandatory control-by-control result list with console outcomes. The keyboard test programmatically focuses one disclosure and presses Enter; it does not establish a complete Tab/Shift+Tab route through every control.

This is a claim/evidence gap, not a finding that those controls are broken or were never manually used. The report's narrow statement that an Enter-key evidence control was tested is accurate.

Proposed correction: replace the universal sentence with an exact statement of the exercised controls. If keeping the universal claim, first record a complete click-through of each distinct changed control, including both return links, relevant states, keyboard traversal, and console errors. Keep automated, manual, and source-inspection evidence distinct.

Acceptance: a reviewer can map every control covered by the sentence to a recorded action and result. Do not manufacture a retrospective test log.

### 2. MEDIUM: Important visual evidence is too small, and the desktop crop cuts off the missing-evidence row

Rules: R-06 (typography must improve readability), C-4 (resilience of the presented evidence). Related quality lock: R-05 (content-driven composition).

Location: pages 7 and 8; screenshot sizing in `assessment/build_report.py`.

One-line reason: Whole-screen images are reduced until the exact evidence the reviewer should inspect is difficult to read inside the PDF.

The page 8 failure image is a 1280-pixel-wide capture placed at 291 PDF points. A 14-pixel UI label becomes approximately 3.18 points in print. The reader can identify a red panel but cannot comfortably read its failure message or inspect the preserved report without opening an external image. Page 7 reduces 1280 pixels to 499 points; comparable UI text is about 5.46 points. Its crop also truncates the fourth requirement, the key not-assessed example.

The captions and full-resolution links are honest and useful. They do not replace legible embedded evidence. No page-text clipping or footer collision was found; this finding concerns image legibility and selection, not broken PDF pagination.

Proposed correction: use a larger crop of the failure message, a separate legible crop of the preserved snapshot/export state, and a crop that includes the unassessed row. Keep an optional overview for context. Label the simulation directly with its figure. Give each screenshot link a distinct name, such as 'Mobile review screenshot' and 'Simulated refresh failure screenshot'.

Acceptance: the benchmark, AI/human source, missing rating, and failure/recovery message can be read in the embedded PDF at a normal full-page reading size. Do not alter screenshot contents to invent evidence.

### 3. LOW: The industry section cites context without explaining an industry insight

Rules: R-16 (specific language), R-31 (write the reason for a decision), C-3 (content-driven composition).

Location: page 3, 'Industry interpretation'; `assessment/build_report.py:125`.

### 4. LOW: Several passages sound like an assistant handing work to the candidate

Acceptance: the PDF consistently explains the work to reviewers; personal study instructions stay in the preparation guide; authorship and verification limits remain explicit.

### 5. LOW: The option comparison makes the selected option too easy to choose

Rule: R-31 (decision reasons must be concrete). Related criterion: C-1 (intentionality).

Location: page 5, options table.

One-line reason: A tiny frontend repair and a wholesale interview-engine replacement bracket the chosen solution, but the table does not expose a close, credible alternative or the chosen option's full costs.

The table meets the brief's basic count of three options and names genuine concerns. This is a quality weakness rather than a missing table. 'Moderate cost', 'substantially greater cost', and 'coherent impact' do not explain what maintenance work remains or what the selected design gives up. The most useful trade-off, a user-triggered lease versus stronger queue guarantees, is explained later instead of being part of the actual decision comparison.

Proposed correction: compare the selected design with at least one plausible intermediate solution. For example, compare refresh correctness using explicit invalidation with digest-based detection, or a lease plus manual retry with a transactional outbox. Explain ownership, deployment burden, reversal cost, and user-visible failures. These are possible analysis directions, not an instruction to invent an evaluation that never occurred.

Acceptance: the selected option wins for stated constraints while admitting a concrete benefit an alternative would provide. No fabricated hours, costs, or research are needed.

### 7. LOW: The two-minute statement compresses a timeout threshold into a recovery guarantee

Rule: R-16 (specific language). Related criterion: C-5 (claims match behavior).

Location: page 10, 'The two-minute lease bounds the commit-to-enqueue gap'; compare the fuller explanation on page 6. Code: `api/app/models/fit_gap_report.rb:6`, `:16`, `:21`, and `FitGap::RequestGeneration`.

One-line reason: Two minutes is the threshold at which an old pending/generating report is exposed as failed, not a promise that a lost job is re-enqueued or completed within two minutes.

Page 6 correctly mentions explicit retry. The short page 10 sentence can still imply automatic bounded recovery. There is no outbox or periodic automatic re-enqueue mechanism in this selected design. Worker/queue delays and the time before a reviewer clicks retry are outside the threshold.

Proposed correction: say, 'After two minutes, an outstanding report is treated as timed out. A reviewer must explicitly retry; recovery time depends on that action and queue availability.' Retain the transactional-outbox trade-off. Describe source-derived failure separately from a persisted failed row if discussing internal status storage.

Acceptance: the PDF distinguishes detection time, recovery action, and eventual completion. The actual implementation does not need to change to make this statement precise.
