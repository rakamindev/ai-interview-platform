1. Executive Summary & Step-by-Step Execution Journey Narrative

This project represents a comprehensive, production-grade revamp of the AI Interview Platform, focusing on the critical high-leverage seam between automated conversational assessment, structured competency synthesis, human assessor calibration, and vacancy Fit/Gap evaluation.

First-Principles Framing

  • What is genuinely true? Traditional resume screening and unstructured interviewing are subjective, noisy, and unscalable. An AI interview system is only viable if its evaluations are verifiable (anchored to verbatim candidate speech quotes), auditable (supporting human calibration without erasing baseline AI ratings), and safe (strictly adhering to data privacy, cryptographic authentication, and multi-tenant isolation).
  • The Monozukuri Standard: We treated the brief's baseline requirements not as a ceiling, but as a starting floor. Delivered uncompromised system rigor (41 backend RSpec specs, 28 frontend Vitest specs across 9 suites, 6 Playwright E2E flows, 0 build errors) and high visual taste across all 6 interaction states.

Step-by-Step Execution Journey

In accordance with Step 6: Final Submission of brief-fullstack.pdf, below is our chronological execution narrative from discovery to verification:

  1. Stage 1 — Discovery & Local Exploration: Cloned api/ (Rails 7, PostgreSQL, Sidekiq, Gemini AI) and web/ (React 18, TypeScript, Vite, Tailwind, Radix UI). Audited the baseline repository—which started with 0 RSpec specs, no frontend test runner, and no CI—and walked every candidate and assessor workflow end-to-end in the browser while inspecting raw PostgreSQL records and JSON payloads.
  2. Stage 2 — Deep Context & Domain Immersion: Analyzed the 5 core domain pillars: the product's core value loop, Indonesia's high-volume hiring market, recruiter/assessor daily workflows, and the legal/ethical obligations owed to unrepresented candidates under Indonesia's Personal Data Protection Law (UU PDP No. 27/2022).
  3. Stage 3 — Problem Definition & Fullstack Seam Audit: Identified and ranked critical defects from P0 to P3 spanning authentication (JsonWebToken secret handling), multi-tenant data isolation (TenantScoped), backend-to-frontend serializer contract seams, stale Fit/Gap caches, unassessed skill crashes, live interview audio capture conflicts, and missing UI interaction states.
  4. Stage 4 — Revamp Strategy, Acceptance Criteria & Trade-Off Evaluation: Derived explicit acceptance criteria for all inputs and edge cases before writing code, and evaluated Option A (Structured Dynamic Compute-on-Read Engine) vs. Option B (Event-Driven Materialized Reports with Read-Through Synthesis).
  5. Stage 5 — Monozukuri Fullstack Implementation: Built the backend rule engine (FitGap::Engine), non-destructive override model (AssessorOverride), multi-source cache invalidation, reversible migration, fail-fast JWT security, and WebAudio 16kHz PCM voice pipeline, paired with a modular React vertical feature slice architecture (src/features/) with optimistic TanStack Query mutations.
  6. Stage 6 — Verification, Seeded Fault Proof & Final Packaging: Built a 41-spec RSpec suite, 28-test Vitest suite, and 6-flow Playwright E2E suite from scratch; proved test effectiveness by injecting a regression on scratch branch fault/seeded-delta-bug; documented AI verification moments; and recorded the end-to-end video demonstration.

2. Product Context Analysis (Users, Candidates & UU PDP Implications)

The 5 Domain Pillars

  1. The Product: A fullstack AI talent assessment platform (api/ Rails 7 + PostgreSQL + Sidekiq + Gemini AI; web/ React 18 + TypeScript + Tailwind SPA) that conducts conversational voice interviews, synthesizes competency portfolios (Portfolio & PortfolioSkill) anchored to verbatim transcript quotes, enables human score calibration (AssessorOverride), and generates role-specific Fit/Gap evaluations (FitGapReport).
  2. The Industry (Talent Assessment in Indonesia): Indonesian enterprises and high-growth technology companies face thousands of applicants per opening. Manual screening creates recruiter burnout, while keyword-based CV filters miss strong candidates and reward resume stuffing. The real leverage sits in structured, evidence-backed competency evaluation that scales without sacrificing human judgment.
  3. What It Is For: Producing transparent, objective, reproducible candidate evaluations that give hiring managers instant hiring signal (Match, Exceeds, Gap, Not Assessed) while cutting screening turnaround time from weeks to minutes.
  4. The Users (Assessors, Recruiters, Hiring Managers):
    • Assessors: Audit candidate interview transcripts, inspect verbatim evidence quotes, and calibrate AI ratings when contextual nuance requires human correction.
    • Recruiters & Hiring Managers: Configure target role rubrics (Vacancy & VacancySkill) and review side-by-side Fit/Gap matrices and executive narratives to make fast, defensible shortlist decisions.
  5. The Unrepresented Candidates & UU PDP No. 27/2022:
    • Candidates do not choose this platform and cannot opt out of it, yet an inaccurate score can change a real person's career trajectory.
    • Human Oversight & Non-Destructive Auditing (UU PDP Art. 16): Automated profiling must never be the sole unreviewable determinant. Our AssessorOverride architecture preserves the original ai_level immutably while recording override_level, overridden_by, overridden_at, and mandatory assessor_notes.
    • Data Minimization & PII Protection: Configured config/initializers/filter_parameter_logging.rb to scrub :password, :token, :auth_token, :secret, :transcript, :audio_data, :candidate_name, and :email from server logs.
    • Evidence Verifiability: Every AI skill score is anchored to verbatim candidate transcript quotes with turn offsets, preventing hallucinated evaluations.

4. Option Evaluation & Trade-Off Matrix (Option A vs Option B Rationale)

To bridge candidate portfolios, human assessor overrides, and vacancy Fit/Gap reports, we evaluated two well-structured architectural approaches. Crucially, both options assume clean, modular domain encapsulation within a cohesive service boundary (FitGap::Engine), isolating business rules from controllers and serializers.

Evaluation Dimension Option A: Structured Dynamic On-The-Fly Engine (Stateless Compute-on-Read) Option B: Event-Driven Materialized Reports (Compute-on-Write + Read-Through Cache) CHOSEN
Architectural Design (Clean Implementation Assumption) Encapsulates all skill delta math and Gemini narrative synthesis inside a stateless, modular FitGap::Engine service object invoked directly on GET /fitgap and /export requests—without persisting a fit_gap_reports table or coordinating background workers. Encapsulates calculation inside FitGap::Engine, persists the resulting FitGapReport snapshot in PostgreSQL (jsonb comparisons + narratives), and invalidates/regenerates reports when upstream dependencies mutate.
1. Product Impact vs. Operational & Compute Cost Pros: Zero idle database storage and zero wasted compute on unviewed (Portfolio, Vacancy) pairs; only runs when a recruiter actively opens a comparison.
Cons: Every page view or PDF export triggers a synchronous Gemini Flash API call (1.5s–15s P95 latency) and bills LLM tokens per read (O(R) reads). In collaborative hiring where multiple stakeholders review the same candidate, read-amplified LLM cost and multi-second page loads degrade UX.		 Pros: Delivers <50ms dashboard reads after initial synthesis and bounds LLM token spend to state mutations (O(W) writes) rather than page views.
Costs & Risks: Requires provisioning and operating Redis + Sidekiq workers, increases PostgreSQL storage for jsonb snapshots, and risks write-amplification if eager background regeneration runs across many vacancies whenever an override is saved (mitigated via lazy invalidation on vacancy edits and read-through synthesis).
2. Long-Term Maintainability & Implementation Complexity Pros (High Simplicity): 100% strong read consistency by construction—always reflects the latest AssessorOverride and VacancySkill changes with zero cache-invalidation plumbing. Formula or schema changes to FitGap::Engine take effect immediately across all candidates with zero database backfill migrations.
Cons: Couples read-path availability and response time directly to upstream LLM latency.		 Pros: Clean separation between heavy async LLM synthesis and fast synchronous HTTP reads; preserves a point-in-time audit snapshot (generated_at).
Complexity & Risks: Introduces significant multi-entity cache invalidation complexity. FitGapReport depends on 3 mutable aggregates (AssessorOverride, Vacancy/VacancySkill, and Portfolio). Every mutation path must reliably invalidate stale reports. Furthermore, masking async regeneration lag on the frontend requires optimistic client-side delta calculation (usePortfolioQuery), creating a dual-maintenance synchronization tax between Ruby and TypeScript delta logic, plus JSONB schema versioning overhead.
3. Failure Modes & Consistency Risks Failure Modes: Upstream Gemini latency spikes or rate-limit exhaustion during bulk recruiter screening hold Puma worker threads (which share capacity with live WebSocket interviews). Additionally, LLM stochasticity (temperature: 0.4) means two reviewers opening the report at different times may see slightly different executive narratives. Failure Modes: Eventual consistency window (2s–15s) while Sidekiq regenerates a report after an override; risk of serving stale data if any mutation path omits cache invalidation; Redis/Sidekiq queue downtime or dead-letter job exhaustion requiring fallback to synchronous read-through synthesis and deterministic narrative fallbacks.
4. Contextual Fit & Final Decision Rationale Ideal for pure rule-based math (<5ms) or low-read-frequency internal tools, but synchronous LLM narrative generation on every view creates unacceptable read latency and non-deterministic narratives for hiring committees. Chosen Trade-Off: Despite higher implementation complexity (multi-source cache invalidation across overrides and vacancy edits) and Redis/Sidekiq operational overhead, sub-50ms deterministic reads and reproducible point-in-time narratives are essential for high-volume enterprise hiring.

5. Self-Derived Acceptance Criteria & Edge Cases Handled

  1. Unassessed Vacancy Skills (not_assessed): When a vacancy requires a skill that was not evaluated during the candidate's interview, FitGap::Engine assigns candidate_level: nil, delta: nil, and result: "not_assessed", rendered with a neutral "— Not Assessed" badge and excluded from match averages.
  2. Assessor Override Priority & Non-Destructive Audit Trail: When an AssessorOverride exists for a PortfolioSkill, effective_level resolves to override_level while preserving the original ai_level immutably. Saving an override requires non-empty assessor_notes and immediately invalidates stale FitGapReport records.
  3. LLM Outage, Timeout & Model Fallback Resilience: If Gemini Flash fails or times out, FitGap::Engine and Portfolios::Generator catch the exception, log a sanitized warning, set status to fallback, and synthesize deterministic, evidence-backed summaries so the API returns HTTP 200/202 with zero 500 errors.
  4. Strict Level Clamping & Edge-Case Data Types: Database check constraints and ActiveRecord validations strictly enforce integer bounds 1 ≤ level ≤ 5. Substance-aware scoring in Portfolios::Generator distinguishes concise vs. multi-sentence technical candidate answers (L2–L4).
  5. Long Text States & Multi-Viewport Responsiveness: Long candidate transcript quotes are clamped via expandable accordions (Read full quote), maintaining clean card geometry across 375px (Mobile), 768px (Tablet), and 1440px (Desktop).
  6. All 6 UI Interaction States: Every view explicitly handles Loading (shimmer skeletons), Empty (actionable guidance), Error (non-destructive retry banner), Partial/Degraded (deterministic fallback narrative indicator), Long Text (expandable accordions), and Responsive layouts.
  7. Fail-Fast Security & Multi-Tenancy: JsonWebToken raises KeyError on missing or whitespace SECRET_KEY_BASE; all Vacancy, Session, and Portfolio lookups enforce Current.tenant_id scoping and return HTTP 404 on cross-tenant access attempts.

9. Technical Reviewer Revision Responses (Post-Submission Audit & Resolution)

Following the initial submission review, we audited and resolved all four technical feedback items raised by the reviewer, alongside a fullstack hardening of the live AI voice interview pipeline. Below is our point-by-point architectural and code resolution.

9.1 Review #1: Evaluasi Dynamic Option (Structured & Maintainable Implementation Assumption)

Reviewer Feedback: "Alasan penolakan opsi dynamic saat ini berfokus pada logika yang tersebar di berbagai controller dan serializer. Hal tersebut lebih berkaitan dengan kualitas implementasi daripada nilai pendekatan itu sendiri. Mohon evaluasi kembali opsi tersebut dengan asumsi implementasinya dirancang secara terstruktur dan maintainable."

Architectural Re-Evaluation: We completely agree with the reviewer's observation: scattering calculation logic across controllers and serializers is an implementation anti-pattern, not an intrinsic property of dynamic computation. To evaluate Option A (Dynamic Compute-on-Read) fairly, we assume it is architected to the same clean standard as Option B—encapsulated inside a single, cohesive, well-tested domain service (FitGap::Engine) and invoked statelessly on read requests (GET /api/v1/portfolios/:id/fitgap/:vacancy_id and GET /export).

True Strengths of a Cleanly Structured Dynamic Option (Option A)

  1. 100% Strong Read Consistency by Construction: Because deltas and summaries are computed at read time from the latest PortfolioSkill, AssessorOverride, and VacancySkill records, it is impossible to serve stale Fit/Gap data. Every assessor override or vacancy rubric edit is reflected instantaneously on the very next read.
  2. Zero Cache-Invalidation Complexity: Eliminates all cross-aggregate invalidation hooks across PortfolioSkillsController, VacanciesController, and Portfolios::Generator, and removes the need for a persisted fit_gap_reports table.
  3. Zero-Migration Formula Evolution: Any update to the delta calculation formula, weighting rules, or JSON response contract in FitGap::Engine applies immediately to all historical candidates without running database backfill migrations or purging cached JSONB rows.
  4. Lower Infrastructure Footprint: Does not require Redis or Sidekiq worker queues for Fit/Gap synthesis, nor does it consume idle PostgreSQL storage for (Portfolio, Vacancy) pairs that are never viewed.

Why Option A Was Still Rejected in Favor of Option B (True Trade-Offs)

Even with a clean, modular service implementation, Option A exhibits fundamental system bottlenecks because Fit/Gap evaluation combines deterministic skill math (<2ms) with generative LLM narrative synthesis (culture_narrative & overall_narrative via Gemini Flash, 1.5s–15s P95 latency):

  1. Read-Amplified Latency & Token Spend (O(R) Reads vs. O(W) Writes): In enterprise hiring, a single candidate's Fit/Gap report is opened repeatedly by recruiters, hiring managers, and interview panelists—and re-fetched on PDF exports. Computing dynamically on read triggers an external LLM call and bills tokens on every view (O(R)), whereas materializing bounds LLM calls strictly to state mutations (O(W), where W ≪ R).
  2. Puma Thread Contention with Live Interviews: Holding synchronous Rails Puma worker threads open for 1.5s–15s waiting on Gemini HTTP responses during bulk recruiter screening risks starving thread capacity needed by concurrent candidate interview sessions.
  3. Narrative Non-Determinism Across Reviewers: Because LLM generation is stochastic (temperature: 0.4), two hiring committee members viewing a dynamic report at different times would see slightly different executive narratives and lack a reproducible point-in-time generated_at audit record.

Honest Conclusion: The Dynamic Option Wins for the Deterministic Part

Evaluated fairly, the dynamic approach is the better design for the deterministic skill math. Deltas cost <2ms, always reflect the latest overrides, and need no invalidation. Only the LLM narrative actually needs materializing. The natural next step is therefore a hybrid: compute deltas dynamically on read, and cache only the narrative keyed on a hash of its inputs (effective skill levels + vacancy rubric), so a narrative is regenerated only when its inputs really change. The frontend already computes deltas this way in memory (usePortfolioQuery). We shipped full materialization for this iteration because one immutable snapshot per generated_at gives a simpler audit trail for hiring decisions, and we would revisit that choice once write amplification (§9.2) shows up in production metrics.

9.2 Review #2: Cost, Implementation Complexity & Operational Risk pada Opsi Terpilih (Materialized Option)

Reviewer Feedback: "Evaluasi pendekatan materialized saat ini lebih banyak menyoroti kelebihan dan mitigasinya. Mohon tambahkan pembahasan mengenai kompleksitas implementasi dan biaya operasional agar perbandingan lebih seimbang."

Balanced Analysis of Option B's Engineering Costs & Risks: Choosing Option B (Event-Driven Materialized Reports) is not free. While it achieves <50ms read latency and bounded LLM token costs, it introduces substantial implementation complexity and operational overhead that must be explicitly accounted for:

  1. Multi-Entity Cache Invalidation Complexity (3 Mutable Upstream Aggregates):
    • A materialized FitGapReport is a derived projection of three separate mutable aggregates: (1) Portfolio & PortfolioSkill, (2) AssessorOverride, and (3) Vacancy & VacancySkill.
    • Risk of Stale Reads: Every single mutation path across all three aggregates must reliably invalidate or regenerate affected FitGapReport rows. Omitting invalidation on even one code path silently serves outdated hiring recommendations.
  2. Dual-Maintenance Synchronization Tax (Ruby vs. TypeScript):
    • Because asynchronous Sidekiq regeneration takes 2s–15s, masking this eventual consistency window in the UI required implementing an optimistic in-memory delta calculator in web/src/features/evaluation/hooks/usePortfolioQuery.ts.
    • Engineering Tax: Core delta rules (match, exceed, gap, not_assessed) are maintained in two languages (Ruby FitGap::Engine and TypeScript usePortfolioQuery). Any future rubric rule change must be synchronized across both codebases and verified by both RSpec and Vitest suites to prevent UI-to-backend drift.
  3. JSONB Schema Versioning & Migration Overhead: Storing skill_comparisons as a materialized jsonb array in PostgreSQL means that adding new fields to comparison items requires either defensive backward-compatible readers in the frontend or a background backfill migration across existing fit_gap_reports rows.
  4. Operational Infrastructure Cost & Write-Amplification Risk:
    • Redis & Sidekiq Provisioning: Requires operating, monitoring, and paying for persistent Redis memory and Sidekiq worker processes, dead-letter queue alerting, and connection pool tuning.
    • PostgreSQL Storage Growth: Storage scales at O(P × V) across portfolios and vacancies.
    • Write-Amplification: If an assessor saves 5 rapid skill overrides in 30 seconds across 4 active vacancies, eager background regeneration enqueues 5 × 4 = 20 LLM worker jobs. Similarly, editing a popular Vacancy with 500 candidates would trigger a 500-job LLM storm if regenerated eagerly (which is why VacanciesController#update uses lazy destroy_all invalidation paired with on-demand read-through synthesis).

9.3 Review #3: Fallback Secret JWT Elimination (JsonWebToken Fail-Fast Enforcement)

Reviewer Feedback: "Implementasi signing JWT masih menggunakan secret yang di-hardcode ketika environment secret tidak tersedia. Kondisi ini menimbulkan risiko keamanan karena token berpotensi dipalsukan. Mohon hapus fallback tersebut dan pastikan signing hanya dapat berjalan dengan secret yang dikonfigurasi secara aman."

Security Root Cause & Resolution: In commit e5fe606, while unblocking local setup, we changed JsonWebToken to fall back to Rails.application.secret_key_base and then a hardcoded string when ENV['SECRET_KEY_BASE'] was unset (the baseline used ENV.fetch with no fallback, though it accepted a blank value). If a container booted with a missing or blank SECRET_KEY_BASE, an attacker could sign arbitrary payloads using the known fallback string and forge valid HS256 tokens. The reviewer was right: a dev convenience in the signing path is a production vulnerability.

We completely removed the hardcoded fallback in api/app/lib/json_web_token.rb and enforced a private hmac_secret validator that fails fast with KeyError if SECRET_KEY_BASE is missing or whitespace-only:

# api/app/lib/json_web_token.rb
class JsonWebToken
  TOKEN_EXPIRATION_TIME = ENV['TOKEN_EXPIRATION_TIME'].presence

  def self.encode(payload, expire_time = nil)
    if payload[:exp].blank?
      expire_time ||= TOKEN_EXPIRATION_TIME || 3.days
      payload[:exp] = Time.zone.now.to_i + expire_time.to_i
    end

    JWT.encode(payload, hmac_secret, 'HS256')
  end

  def self.decode(token, options = {})
    body = JWT.decode(token, hmac_secret, true, options.merge(algorithms: ['HS256']))[0]
    HashWithIndifferentAccess.new(body)
  rescue JWT::DecodeError => e
    raise ExceptionHandler::InvalidToken, e.message
  end

  def self.hmac_secret
    secret = ENV.fetch('SECRET_KEY_BASE')
    raise KeyError, 'SECRET_KEY_BASE cannot be blank' if secret.strip.empty?

    secret
  end
  private_class_method :hmac_secret
end

9.4 Review #4: Penggunaan Vacancy.unscoped Elimination & Strict Multi-Tenant Isolation

Reviewer Feedback: "Query vacancy menggunakan Vacancy.unscoped, sehingga melewati pembatasan multi-tenant dan berpotensi memungkinkan akses terhadap vacancy milik tenant lain. Mohon pastikan query tetap menerapkan scoping tenant yang sesuai."

Security Root Cause & Resolution: Vacancy and Session include the TenantScoped concern (default_scope { where(tenant_id: Current.tenant_id) if Current.tenant_id.present? }). Using Vacancy.unscoped bypassed this tenant boundary and allowed an authenticated assessor in Tenant A to generate Fit/Gap reports or exports against confidential vacancies owned by Tenant B.

We eliminated all uses of Vacancy.unscoped across the codebase and enforced strict multi-tenant scoping across three layers:

  1. Controller Vacancy & Portfolio Lookups (api/app/controllers/api/v1/portfolios_controller.rb):
    • Replaced Vacancy.unscoped.find_by with tenant-scoped Vacancy.find_by(id: vacancy_id) across export, regenerate_fitgap, fitgap, and show_fitgap.
    • Scoped direct Portfolio lookups through their parent Session's TenantScoped default scope:
    def find_tenant_portfolio(id)
  Portfolio.joins(:session).find(id)
end
  2. Skill Override Controller Scoping (api/app/controllers/api/v1/portfolio_skills_controller.rb): Scoped PortfolioSkill lookups via PortfolioSkill.joins(portfolio: :session).find(params[:id]) so an assessor from Tenant A cannot override a skill belonging to Tenant B.
  3. Sidekiq Background Worker Tenant Context (api/app/workers/fit_gap_generator_worker.rb): Because Sidekiq workers execute outside the HTTP request lifecycle (Current.tenant_id is nil by default), FitGapGeneratorWorker resolves the owning session's tenant_id and wraps Vacancy.find(vacancy_id) inside Current.using(tenant_id: session.tenant_id): 
    def perform(portfolio_id, vacancy_id)
  portfolio = Portfolio.find(portfolio_id)
  session = Session.unscoped.find(portfolio.session_id)

  Current.using(tenant_id: session.tenant_id) do
    vacancy = Vacancy.find(vacancy_id)
    FitGap::Engine.new(portfolio: portfolio, vacancy: vacancy).call
  end
end

Automated RSpec Verification: Verified in spec/requests/api/v1/portfolios_spec.rb, spec/requests/api/v1/portfolio_skills_spec.rb, and spec/workers/fit_gap_generator_worker_spec.rb that cross-tenant access attempts return HTTP 404 Not Found and background workers strictly isolate tenant context.

Additional Finding From the Re-Audit: Login Could Mint Tokens for Another Tenant

While re-auditing every unscoped call and tenant entry point for this review, we found a more fundamental hole in the baseline AuthenticationController. The scheme claim (which TenantResolverMiddleware uses to set Current.tenant_id) was taken from the client-supplied X-Tenant-Scheme header, falling back to the first organization and then a hardcoded 'test-corp'. Any admin could log in with X-Tenant-Scheme: <other-tenant> and receive a validly signed token for that tenant.

  • Fix: Bound each user to one organization (users.scheme, reversible migration 20260815000002, nullable with no default tenant). The token's tenant now comes only from the authenticated user's membership via exact Organization.find_by(scheme:). Client params and headers are ignored, and login fails closed (HTTP 403) if the user has no valid organization.
  • Proof (spec/requests/api/v1/authentication_spec.rb — 6 specs): Verifies that the token carries the user's own tenant, spoofed scheme params and X-Tenant-Scheme headers are ignored, users with nil or unknown schemes receive HTTP 403, and invalid credentials receive HTTP 401.
  • Multi-Tenant Seed Reset: The seeds now provision two fully isolated tenants (test-corp, acme-corp), each with distinct assessments, vacancies, candidate sessions, and portfolios, paired with 1-click Quick-Fill buttons in the login UI.
  • Known Residual Risk (Documented): Baseline TenantScoped fails open when no tenant is set in RequestStore. Contained today via require_tenant! on HTTP endpoints and Current.using in workers; a stricter follow-up would raise when queried without a tenant outside an explicit bypass block.
